EXPORT PROCESS · PACKING & SHIPPING

How a core drilling rig is packed and shipped in a 40 ft container

Machinery export lives or dies at the packing stage. This walkthrough follows the actual preparation of drilling equipment for container loading in our workshop — the same steps covered by our shipment examples.

Step by step

  1. Pre-packing inspection & photos. Machine is run, inspected and photographed. Serial-number photos become part of the loading evidence set.
  2. Disassembly to transport configuration. Mast folded or detached, drill rods bundled, hoses capped — every part tagged so reassembly on site is mechanical, not a puzzle.
  3. Fixing inside the container. Steel frames, wooden supports and tension straps fix the power unit and mast so nothing shifts at sea. Heavy items go to the floor, Consumables ride above.
  4. Moisture protection. Desiccant bags, VCI film on machined surfaces and sealed control boxes — a 30-day sea voyage is harder on electronics than a year on site.
  5. Loading evidence & documents. Container number, seal number, loading photos and a packing list that matches the commercial invoice line by line. Buyers receive the full evidence set before the vessel sails.
  6. Spare parts & first-consumables box. Wear parts, filters and a recommended first-campaign consumables list ship in the same container, positioned for easy access on arrival.

What the buyer should prepare

  • Confirm destination port and inland route — see the transport and site access checklist
  • Check import requirements for used/new machinery in your country; our certificate portfolio covers compliance documentation
  • Agree unloading equipment at site (crane or forklift capacity for the power unit)
