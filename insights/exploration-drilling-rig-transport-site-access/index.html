Exploration Drilling Rig Transport and Site Access Checklist

A buyer checklist for transport dimensions, access routes, setup footprint and utilities before choosing a crawler core rig.

Published September 24, 2026

A buyer checklist for transport dimensions, access routes, setup footprint and utilities before choosing a crawler core rig. Use the checklist below to compare a quoted configuration with the demands of your site.

Measure the route before selecting the rig

A rig that suits the hole still has to reach the drilling pad. Document gate width, bridge limits, road gradient, turning room, overhead clearance and the distance from unloading to the hole. The narrowest point in the route usually matters more than the open space around the final pad.

Separate transport and operating dimensions

Ask for the folded transport length, width, height and weight of the specific proposed configuration. Then request the mast-up working envelope and clearance for rod handling. The FDX600, FD1000 and FD1500 should be compared on their individual model pages and current quotation, including installed options. A transport drawing for a different rig can lead to an expensive route mistake.

Include the support equipment

The rig is only one part of a working site. Allow room for rods, core trays, water or fluid equipment, tools, fuel or power supply, and people to work around the machine. A narrow pad can create handling problems even if the crawler itself fits. Ask how the mast is raised and where service doors open.

Account for unloading and ground conditions

Confirm the agreed loading arrangement, lifting points, ramps or handling equipment and actual shipping mass. Verify the ground can support the machine and the planned unloading method; the route may change after rain. Site access and setup decisions belong in the project lift and traffic plan, not in an assumption based on a catalogue photograph.

Send a useful site sketch

A simple sketch can show access width, turning radii, slope, obstacles and proposed hole position. Add photos and note any local vehicle limits. Request a configuration-specific transport drawing and a written accessories list before booking freight or committing to a pad design.
