How to Choose an Exploration Drilling Rig for Core Drilling

The right exploration drilling rig is not simply the model with the greatest stated depth. A dependable selection starts with the core program, expected formation, site access and the support needed after delivery.

Updated September 23, 2026

Start with the real drilling program

Confirm the target depth, expected final hole diameter and core standard before comparing machines. BTW, NTW and HTW capacities are different because rod diameter, hole size and load increase. A rig rated to a certain depth in one core size may have a lower capacity in a larger size. Include a reasonable reserve instead of operating continuously at the published limit.

Match torque and speed to the formation

Hard competent rock generally benefits from stable high-speed coring, while broken formations can require controlled torque, careful feed pressure and suitable mud circulation. Ask the supplier to explain the usable rotary-speed range, maximum torque, feed stroke, pullback force and pressure-control method—not only engine power.

Check terrain, mast and transport limits

Crawler type, gradeability, machine width and transport height determine whether the rig can reach the site. Confirm the working footprint, mast height, drilling-angle range and outrigger space. For remote projects, check whether local cranes, low-bed trailers or containers can handle the packed dimensions and weight.

Review the complete drilling system

A productive project depends on more than the base rig. Confirm the mud pump, wireline winch, hydraulic clamp, rod handling, cooling system, tooling and recommended spare parts. The engine, voltage and cold-weather or high-temperature configuration should match the destination market.

Ask for written confirmation before ordering

Request a final configuration sheet showing drilling capacity by core size, engine and power, pump, dimensions, weight, included tools, optional items and delivery scope. Written confirmation reduces misunderstandings and gives both sides a clear basis for inspection before shipment.
