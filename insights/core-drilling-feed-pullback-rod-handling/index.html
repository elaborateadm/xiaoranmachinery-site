Core Drilling Rig Feed, Pullback and Rod Handling: What to Compare

A practical comparison of feed travel, pullback capability, rod handling and recovery planning for wireline exploration rigs.

Published September 24, 2026

A practical comparison of feed travel, pullback capability, rod handling and recovery planning for wireline exploration rigs. Use the checklist below to compare a quoted configuration with the demands of your site.

Why nominal drilling depth is only a starting point

A depth figure is useful for screening rigs, but it does not describe how the machine advances the string, retrieves it or handles a difficult hole. The FDX400 exploration rig and FDX600 exploration rig belong to different depth classes; ask for the complete configuration and the rod system behind each published rating before comparing them. Formation, hole diameter, angle, tooling and circulation conditions all change the work required at a given depth.

Feed travel and the drilling cycle

Feed stroke is the distance the head can move in one advance. A longer stroke may reduce the number of resets for a given rod length, but the transport mast, setup space and chosen rods must fit the site. Compare usable feed travel with the actual rod length and any casing plan. Ask whether the stated feed force, pullback force and spindle torque are measured under the same operating conditions; a single large number does not tell the entire story.

Pullback and a stuck-string plan

Pullback matters when raising a long string or responding to binding. It is not a promise that any stuck string can be freed. For a deeper program, compare the FD800 model with the planned rod weight, hole geometry, winch arrangement and handling procedure. Build a recovery plan with an experienced drilling team before mobilization, including suitable tooling and a realistic limit for the rig and rods.

Rod handling and crew access

Record whether rods will be handled manually or with optional mechanized assistance. Check the gripping range, rod clamps, chuck, winch and staging space around the machine. Confirm how the mast position affects rod handling on angled holes. For remote work, spare clamps, seals, hoses and compatible couplings can matter as much as nominal drilling capacity.

Questions to send with a quotation request

Provide target depth and hole size, expected geology, drilling angle, planned rods, water availability, transport restrictions and crew arrangement. Ask for a configuration list that identifies power unit, pump, tooling, rod handling and options individually. Compare a rig only after those assumptions match your proposed job.
