Crawler Water Well Rig for Sale: 7 Checks Before You Buy

Seven checks before buying a crawler water well rig: depth and diameter, drilling method, rod handling, transport, engine, parts support and supplier.

Published November 27, 2026

Most buyers comparing a crawler water well rig for sale are choosing between machines that look alike in a photograph and differ in ways that only show up on site. The seven checks below are the ones that decide whether a rig drills your hole, reaches your site and keeps working. Each one ends in a number you can ask for in writing.

Quick answer: Confirm depth and diameter against your aquifer, choose the drilling method before the rig, then check feed stroke, transport width, engine and parts support. Ask for every figure in the written quotation so the machine you order matches the hole you need.

1. Depth and diameter against your aquifer

Rated depth means little on its own. What matters is the depth at which a rig can hold your required hole diameter in your formation. A machine advertised at 300 m may reach that depth only at its smallest diameter; open the hole to carry a larger casing and the same rig reaches less. Start from the completion — borehole diameter, casing size and pump — and work back to the water well drilling rig that can deliver it.

2. Decide the method before the rig

Air (DTH) and mud rotary suit different ground, and the choice changes the whole package: an air rig needs a matched compressor, while a mud rig needs a pump, a pit and fluid handling. Decide the method from your formation first, then make sure the rig and its air or mud package are quoted together rather than as separate lines. Our compressor matching guide sets out how pressure and air volume are chosen for the hammer and hole size.

3. Feed stroke, pullback and rod handling

A longer feed stroke means fewer rod joints per metre and faster trips; short-stroke rigs need more handling and more crew time. Check the pullback force and the breakout capacity as well, because a rig that drills the hole but cannot break a tight joint costs you hours. On a 200 m bore it is often the rod-handling cycle, not the drilling rate, that sets the day’s metres.

4. Transport, access and site footprint

A crawler rig buys traction and flotation at the price of a slow move between holes; a truck-mounted rig moves fast but needs a road. Measure the narrowest gate, the turning route and the ground before you buy, then compare those figures with the rig’s transport width and working footprint. On the tightest sites the constraint is the same one that sends buyers to compact lifting gear: folded width, turning radius and ground bearing. XIAORAN builds both sides of that problem, the drilling lines and, for confined-space work on the same kind of site, the compact cranes behind our work as a spider crane manufacturer.

5. Engine, hydraulics and consumables

Record the engine make, rated power and cooling arrangement; a rig working in 40 °C heat needs a cooling package that suits it. Check the hydraulic system pressure and flow, and where the filters and coolers sit for servicing. Fuel burn per shift is a real operating number, so ask for it rather than assuming the headline engine size settles the question.

6. Spare parts, wear items and service access

Wear items decide downtime: hammers and bits, rod threads, hoses, seals and the feed system. Ask what is stocked, what ships from the factory and how long that takes. Good service access — filters, greasing points and the compressor or pump — is worth more in the field than a lower headline figure, and a factory that builds a range of water well drilling rigs for export can usually supply the parts and documentation that keep one of them running.

7. The supplier behind the machine

Finally, look at the buyer behind the brochure. Ask for the specification, the packing plan and the parts list in writing, and check that the depth, diameter and method in the quotation match the hole you described. A supplier that issues written configuration proposals, packs for container shipping and offers pre-order technical consultation is easier to hold to the specification than one that quotes from a photograph.

Frequently asked questions

Is a crawler rig or a truck-mounted rig better for water wells?

It depends on the sites, not the machines. A crawler rig wins on soft or uneven ground and on sites with no road, while a truck-mounted rig wins on programmes that move between many holes spaced along roads. If your work mixes both, that trade-off decides the model more than any single specification.

How much does a crawler water well rig cost?

Cost tracks depth rating, hole diameter, the air or mud package and the accessories added, so a single figure is misleading. Compare quotations against the same completion: the same hole drilled by a smaller rig with a bigger compressor can cost less to run than an oversized rig. Exact pricing and lead time are confirmed in the written quotation.

What should be included in the quotation?

The written quotation should state the rig model, rated depth and diameter, engine rating, the air or mud package, rod type, the wear items supplied and the packing method for shipping. Ask for the casing and screen sizes that match the completion you plan as well.
