Borehole Development After Drilling: Steps and Tools

Surging, air lifting, jetting, gravel pack, pump testing and disinfection: the steps that turn a drilled borehole into a working water well.

Published November 22, 2026

Drilling the hole is only half of a water well. The borehole that comes out of the ground is usually surrounded by a filter cake of drilling fines and loose material that will keep feeding sand into the well unless it is removed deliberately. Development removes those fines, stabilises the annulus and measures what the hole can actually yield.

Quick answer: Develop with surging or air lifting first, then jetting, then set the gravel pack and screen, then carry out a measured pump test, and finish with disinfection. Skipping a step usually shows up later as sand production or falling yield.

What development is actually removing

A rotary or DTH hole is drilled with a fluid or an air stream that carries cuttings out of the annulus, and some of that material stays behind. Two things in particular hurt the finished well:

  • The filter cake. Drilling fluid deposits a thin layer of clay and fines on the bore wall. Left in place behind a screen, it blocks the formation face and holds back water.
  • Loose fines in the aquifer. Sand and silt-size particles sit in the pores around the hole. When pumping starts, they migrate toward the well and either enter it or pack tighter and reduce permeability close to the bore.

Development breaks down the filter cake and draws the mobile fines out of the near-well zone, so the formation re-sorts into a stable, more permeable envelope. That is why it is timed and measured rather than treated as a rinse.

Step 1 — Surging and air lifting

Surging is a cycle of short, sharp flow reversals that flexes the screen and the formation behind it, loosening fines lodged against the openings. Air lifting does the same job with a continuous air stream: air is injected down the string and returns water and fines up the annulus until the water runs clear.

An airlift campaign is normally run intermittently — pump until the discharge starts carrying fines, stop, let the formation settle, then repeat. The interval is judged by what comes up, not by a fixed clock. On a water well rig that already has an air package this is the cheapest development method available, because it uses the same compressor that drilled the hole. The pressure and volume that package must supply are covered in our DTH compressor matching guide.

Step 2 — Jetting and high-pressure washing

Jetting directs a high-pressure stream of water down through a wash pipe with opposed nozzles, washing the formation face directly. It is more directional than surging and is useful where a specific interval is producing fines, or where the screen interval is long and surging is not reaching the whole length. Work interval by interval from the bottom up, checking the return water at each stage, and keep jetting pressures inside what the screen and casing can take.

Step 3 — Gravel pack and screen placement

Where the aquifer will not stabilise on its own, a graded gravel pack placed around the screen acts as a filter: it holds back the formation sand while letting water through. The pack's grading is chosen against the formation grain size, and the screen slot against the pack. The table below sets out how the common development methods differ.

MethodBest used whenWhat it doesMain caution
SurgingScreen and casing already set, moderate finesFlexes screen and formation, loosens lodged finesAggressive cycling can move a thin gravel pack
Air liftingAir package already on siteContinuous removal of fines until discharge runs clearNeeds air pressure and volume matched to depth
JettingLong screen intervals, localised finesDirect wash of the formation face, interval by intervalPressure must stay within screen and casing limits
Gravel packFine or running sand aquifersActs as a graded filter around the screenGrading must be selected from the formation grain size
Pump testAfter development is completeMeasures yield and drawdown for acceptanceA short test cannot show sustainable yield

Method selection is site-specific. Confirm the completion design, pack grading and screen specification for your formation before ordering.

Step 4 — Pump testing and yield measurement

A well is accepted on measured yield, not on how the water looks. A straightforward test sequence covers three stages:

StagePurposeRecords to keep
Cleaning / step testBring the hole to stable, sand-free dischargeTime, pumping rate, water clarity, sand content
Constant-rate testEstablish yield against drawdown at one rateRate, water level at each interval, stabilised drawdown
Recovery measurementShow the aquifer refills as expectedWater level over time after pumping stops

Water levels should be recorded at short intervals early in the test and at longer intervals once the level stabilises. The recovery curve matters as much as the drawdown: a hole that draws down slowly but recovers slowly is telling you something different from one that stabilises quickly at the same rate. The test also fixes the pump setting depth, which should come from the measured drawdown with margin rather than from the drilled depth.

Step 5 — Disinfection and handover

Once development and testing are complete, the well and its headworks are disinfected before handover, following the procedure and dosing specified for the project. Record the date of disinfection, the method and any water sample results in the handover file, together with the drilling log, construction record, development log and pump test data. Without that record a later problem is very hard to diagnose.

Tools and consumables to plan for

Development usually borrows the rig that drilled the hole, so the extra items are modest but easy to forget: a wash pipe and jetting nozzles sized to the screen interval, a water level meter with enough cable for the full depth, a means of measuring discharge rate, and a settling pit so return water can be inspected.

Frequently asked questions

How long should development take?

It depends on how much fines the hole produces, not on depth alone. A clean, well-sorted aquifer may develop in a few hours of surging or air lifting; a clay-and-fines hole can take several days of intermittent work. Plan development as its own line in the drilling schedule.

When can development start?

Only after the hole is stable and the casing or screen is in its final position. Developing before the screen is set risks moving fines behind the screen or collapsing the formation. If the hole is running sand, stabilise it first and confirm the completion design and gravel pack grading in the written quotation.

Do I need a pump test before acceptance?

Yes. A borehole is normally accepted on a documented yield test: a measured pumping rate held for a stated period with drawdown recorded over time. Accepting a hole on a short pump-in test alone leaves the sustainable yield unknown and the pump setting depth unverified.
