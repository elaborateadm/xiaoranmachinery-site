Skid Steer Rated Operating Capacity, Bucket Load and Attachment Weight

How to compare rated load, material density and attachment mass when choosing a compact tracked loader.

Published September 24, 2026

How to compare rated load, material density and attachment mass when choosing a compact tracked loader. Use the checklist below to compare a quoted configuration with the demands of your site.

Understand which load figure is being quoted

Rated operating capacity is a machine rating for a defined test and configuration. It is different from operating weight, bucket volume and tipping load. Do not translate a bucket volume into a safe material mass without considering material density and the installed attachment. Confirm the standard and configuration used for any quoted capacity.

Include the attachment in the task

The C1200 Series page lists a 440 kg rated load. An attachment, coupler and any carried material create a combined operating demand; the work must remain within the manufacturer-approved limits for the actual machine and attachment. The EV1000 is another compact loader option, but compare its current rated load and attachments rather than inferring a rating from its name.

Compare lift geometry and ground

Think about where the load is carried and deposited: pallet height, truck side, ramp and turning space. A load extended far forward can feel different from one kept close to the machine. Soft or uneven ground and tight turns also change the practical job plan. Use the operator manual and the approved attachment configuration for the actual operating limit.

Match hydraulics and mounting

For powered attachments, ask for required flow and pressure as well as coupler and electrical controls. A mechanically fitting attachment may still need an unsuitable flow or a control kit. The buying decision should include the attachment weight, working width and service parts alongside hydraulic compatibility.

Build a comparison worksheet

List material and density, planned bucket or tool, attachment mass, dump height, passage width, surface and daily duty. Request the same information for each proposed loader. If the payload is near a stated limit, choose a configuration only after checking the actual manufacturer instructions and site conditions.
