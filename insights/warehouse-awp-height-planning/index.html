Planning Platform Height for Warehouse Racking Work

The 2 m gap between platform height and working height — and why it decides which lift a racking job actually needs.

Published November 25, 2026

Warehouse platform work usually starts from the wrong number — the ceiling height or the top of the racking — when the figure that decides the machine is the working height the operator actually needs. Get that wrong by 2 m and you either overspend on a taller lift or stop short of the top beam.

Quick answer: working height is platform height plus about 2.0 m of operator reach. Match it to the highest point the operator must touch, not to the ceiling. Then check that the rated load covers two workers and their tools, and that the machine's overall width and folded height can reach and pass the aisle. For most racking maintenance a platform height of 8 m to 10 m (GTJZ08 or GTJZ10) covers the task.

Working height is not platform height

Every figure on our specification sheets comes in a pair. The GTJZ06 lists an 8.0 m maximum working height against a 6.0 m maximum platform height; the GTJZ10 lists 12.0 m against 10.0 m; the GTJZ12 lists 13.8 m against 11.8 m. The 2.0 m difference is the operator's reach above the deck. When you size a machine you are choosing the platform height that puts the working height where the task is.

The table only works if you read the third column. A rack top beam at 10 m is reached by a machine whose platform sits at 8 m, not at 10 m — the GTJZ08, not the GTJZ10. Choosing on rack height alone is the most common reason warehouse teams order one size too tall.

If the task is not a fixed work face but a comparison of two similar machines, our guide to choosing between a 10 m and a 12 m platform height walks through the same decision from the model side.

How many people and how much load

Rated load varies by model and is as much a task decision as the height. The compact GTJZ04 and GTJZ045 carry 230 kg, the GTJZ06 and GTJZ08 carry 450 kg, the GTJZ10 and GTJZ12 carry 320 kg, and the tallest GTJZ14 carries 230 kg. The extension deck is rated separately at about 100–113 kg and applies when a worker stands on the roll-out deck beyond the main platform.

For two installers at roughly 80 kg each plus tool bags and a few fixtures, the 450 kg models leave a comfortable margin, while a 230 kg model is realistically a single-operator machine with hand tools. The SJYZ-1012 stands apart at 680 kg with a 1,500 mm platform extension and a stated maximum of two workers, which makes it the model to specify when a maintenance cart or spare parts travel up with the crew.

Reaching the aisle: width, folded height and turning

A machine that fits the load and the height but not the aisle is no use. Overall width runs from 0.78 m on the GTJZ04 and GTJZ045, through 1.15 m on the GTJZ06, GTJZ08 and GTJZ10, to 1.19 m on the GTJZ12 and 1.39 m on the GTJZ14. Folded transport height — the figure that decides whether the machine clears a roller door — is 1.62 m to 2.10 m across the range. Where aisles are genuinely tight, the sub-metre models will pass between racking, though at 230 kg rated load they suit one operator and light tools.

Also plan the turn, not just the straight run. The compact wheeled models use tight steering geometry, but a tracked model such as the GTJZ12 Crawler turns in the length of its own chassis at low speed, which helps at the end of a rack row where there is no room to swing a wheeled machine.

Floor loading and indoor power

Machine weights for the indoor battery range run from 940 kg on the GTJZ04 to 3,600 kg on the GTJZ14, and that load passes through four wheels rather than spreading across the deck, so the point load under a tyre can exceed a warehouse slab limit on upper floors and mezzanines. Before driving a lift onto a mezzanine, check the slab against the machine's concentrated wheel load; our guide to calculating floor load before driving a lift indoors works through the arithmetic.

All the GTJZ models and the HC0607EA are battery-electric with zero local emissions, which is what makes them usable inside a closed warehouse without ventilation, and pothole protection is standard across the range so the platform stops dropping if a wheel enters a gap. Confirm the exact battery type, charge voltage and charger supplied with the model in the written quotation.

Frequently asked questions

What platform height do I need for 8 m racking?

A rack top beam at 8 m needs a working height of about 8 m, which corresponds to a platform height of about 6 m — a GTJZ06 or GTJZ08. If the operator also has to reach above the top beam or work down the face of the rack, add that extra reach and step up to the next model. Size on the highest point the operator must touch, never on the ceiling.

What is the difference between platform height and working height?

Working height is platform height plus roughly 2.0 m of operator reach. On our battery scissor lifts the gap is consistently 2.0 m: the GTJZ06 is 8.0 m working over 6.0 m platform, the GTJZ10 is 12.0 m over 10.0 m, and the GTJZ12 is 13.8 m over 11.8 m. Size the machine on working height, then confirm the platform height in the data sheet.

How much can a scissor lift carry indoors?

Rated load depends on the model: 450 kg on the GTJZ06 and GTJZ08, 320 kg on the GTJZ10 and GTJZ12, 230 kg on the GTJZ04, GTJZ045 and GTJZ14, and 680 kg on the SJYZ-1012. The extension deck is rated separately at about 100–113 kg. Rated load covers people, tools and materials together. Confirm the figure for your model in the written quotation.
