Cobra Crane vs Spider Crane: Mini Crawler Crane Names

Three names for one machine category — and why the name you search with decides which specifications you end up comparing.

Published November 24, 2026

Search for a compact lifting machine and three names come back for the same class of equipment: cobra crane, spider crane and mini crawler crane. Buyers comparing quotations often treat them as different machine types, then compare the wrong numbers.

Quick answer: cobra crane, spider crane and mini crawler crane are three names for the same compact lifting category — a crawler-mounted crane with splayed outrigger legs, a multi-section hydraulic boom and wireless remote control. The name tells you nothing about capacity, so compare model, published rated load, boom length, stabiliser footprint and transport dimensions instead.

Three names, three different points of reference

Each term describes the machine from a different angle, so searching one and then the other returns different pages, suppliers and price brackets for the same equipment.

  • Cobra crane is a trade name, not a machine category. Some manufacturers use a series name of this kind in certain markets, and buyers carry it into search engines years later. It says which brand family someone remembers, not what the machine can lift.
  • Spider crane describes the working geometry. The four stabiliser legs splay outward like spider legs, so a narrow body can be levelled and supported in a courtyard, atrium or rooftop plant room where a truck crane cannot set up. Lifting standards and supplier catalogues use this term for the class.
  • Mini crawler crane describes the chassis: crawler tracks and compact overall size. Tracked mini cranes exist without outrigger legs and lift from the tracks themselves, which changes what the machine can lift indoors and what it does to a finished floor.

The short answer: the name changes what your search returns and which supplier you talk to. It does not change the physics of the lift.

What actually defines the category

Strip the names away and the technical definition is consistent across the range we build: a crawler undercarriage travelling at 0–2.0 to 0–2.5 km/h, folding stabilisers with published minimum and maximum outrigger spans, a four to six section hydraulic boom luffing through 0–80°, continuous 360° slewing, and wireless remote control as standard. Power is a diesel engine or a 220 V / 380 V electric motor, which is what makes the class usable inside a building.

The defining figures are the stabiliser spans, because they set the smallest working space — not the headline capacity. Our guide to outrigger footprint and ground bearing covers how the two interact.

Where the naming causes real procurement mistakes

  • Comparing headline capacity across differently named machines. A 3,000 kg rated load is not a 3,000 kg lift at every radius. Ask for load at your working radius, not the bare maximum.
  • Assuming a mini crawler crane has outriggers. Tracked mini cranes that lift from the tracks distribute load differently and may be ruled out on a suspended floor or finished screed. Confirm the support arrangement, not the label.
  • Assuming a trade name guarantees compatibility. Parts, jibs and suction attachments are model-specific: quote the exact model and confirm the mounting interface before ordering an attachment.

Frequently asked questions

Are cobra cranes and spider cranes the same machine?

They refer to the same class of compact crawler crane. "Cobra crane" is a trade name used for this equipment by some manufacturers in certain markets, while "spider crane" describes the splayed stabiliser geometry that defines the category. Neither term is a technical specification, so a quotation should always be read on model number, rated load, boom length and stabiliser footprint.

Why does a mini crawler crane cost less than a spider crane of the same capacity?

Price differences between machines sold under different names usually come from configuration rather than from the name: boom sections, stabiliser arrangement, power source, control options and the accessories included. A tracked mini crane without outrigger legs is a different configuration from a stabiliser-based spider crane, even at similar rated load. Ask for the configuration list and confirm price, payment terms and warranty in the written quotation.

How do I know which of the three names to search for?

Search the term you know and treat the results as the same category, then evaluate on published data: rated load, boom length, minimum and maximum outrigger span, transport dimensions and power options. If a supplier cannot publish those figures for the exact model, the name on the page will not help you plan the lift.
