Casing and Gravel Pack: Protecting Your Well for Decades

How casing and gravel pack are matched to a water well: casing diameter and material, pack grading from sieve data, screen slots and installation order.

Published November 27, 2026

A drilled borehole rarely stays open on its own, and an open hole is not yet a water well. Casing holds the hole open, seals out shallow contamination and carries the screen and pump; the gravel pack around that screen filters the aquifer so the well gives clear water instead of sand. Both are decided before drilling starts and slow to change afterwards.

Quick answer: Fix the casing diameter from one chain — borehole, casing outside diameter, screen, then pump — and design the gravel pack from a sieve analysis of the formation, sizing the screen slot to the pack rather than to the raw sand.

The three jobs casing has to do

Casing is doing three separate jobs at once, and a choice that suits one can hurt another.

  • Hold the hole open. It resists the collapse pressure of the formation and keeps the bore from caving between the surface and the screen.
  • Seal off the wrong water. A surface string, correctly grouted, stops shallow groundwater, surface run-off and contamination running down the annulus into the aquifer.
  • Carry the completion. The screen, the gravel pack and the pump all sit inside or hang from the casing. Its inside diameter sets the largest pump the well can ever take.

Size casing from one chain, not one number

Buyers often arrive with a single figure — “a 6-inch well” — but the useful figure is a chain. The rig must drill a borehole wide enough for the casing plus the annular space the gravel pack needs. The casing inside diameter must clear the pump body, its cable and any safety rope. The screen must pass the same water the pump moves. Change one link and the others move with it.

The borehole itself is the rig’s responsibility, so the diameter and depth you can build decide which water well drilling rig fits the plan. A rig that drills a 250 mm hole to 200 m cannot deliver the same completion as one rated for a deeper, wider bore.

Casing material and wall thickness

Three families cover most water wells, and the choice turns on water chemistry, depth and handling rather than fashion.

  • Mild steel — the default for deeper or larger wells. Wall thickness is chosen for collapse and tensile load during installation as well as for corrosion allowance over the design life.
  • uPVC — light, easy to handle and immune to the electrochemical corrosion that attacks steel, but lower in strength; usually restricted to shallower, smaller-diameter wells.
  • Stainless steel — used where aggressive water or a long design life justifies the cost, most often for screens.

Joint type matters as much as the material: a threaded joint must both seal and carry load, and a poorly made joint is where a string fails.

Designing the gravel pack in three steps

A gravel pack is an engineered filter, and filters are designed from data. The order is always the same.

  1. Sieve the formation. A representative sample of the aquifer sand is sieved to find its grain-size distribution. Without this, any pack design is a guess.
  2. Pick the pack grading. For most water-bearing sands the pack is chosen so its D50 is roughly four to six times the formation D50, with a low uniformity coefficient so the material is well sorted.
  3. Size the screen slot to the pack. The slot is set from the pack’s finer fraction, not from the formation. A slot that is too wide passes pack; one that is too narrow throttles the well.

Installing so the pack does not bridge

A correct design still fails if it is installed badly. Two habits prevent most problems: centralise the screen so the annular gap is even all the way round, and place the pack slowly from the bottom up with a tremie or wash pipe rather than dumping it. Free-falling gravel segregates, the coarse fraction runs ahead and the pack bridges, leaving sections of screen unprotected. Afterwards, surge or air-lift the well to settle and re-sort the pack — the same development steps that clean the formation also confirm the pack. The rig that drilled the hole usually does this work too, because most water well drilling rigs already carry the air package used for lifting.

Frequently asked questions

How thick should the gravel pack be?

Common practice is a 50–75 mm annular thickness around the screen, but the number follows the formation and the pack grading. A pack that is too thin bridges and stops filtering; one that is far too thick wastes borehole diameter the pump could have used.

Can I complete a well without a gravel pack?

In a well-cemented, stable formation the screen can sometimes sit against the bore wall with no artificial pack, and the aquifer is developed naturally. In fine, running or poorly sorted sand the pack is what stands between you and a well that pumps sand. The completion design in your written quotation should state which approach applies.

Does a bigger casing diameter mean more water?

Not by itself. Yield comes from the aquifer and from how well the screen and pack are matched to it. Casing diameter mainly sets the pump size and the storage inside the well, so oversizing buys a bigger pump and a wider borehole to drill — not automatically more water.
