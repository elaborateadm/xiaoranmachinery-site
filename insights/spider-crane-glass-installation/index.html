Glass Panel Installation With Spider Cranes: Radius, Suction and Lift Planning

Plan glass panel lifts with a spider crane: panel weight and radius math, suction lifter selection, outrigger setup and access route checks..

Published November 19, 2026

Why spider cranes suit glass work

Curtain-wall panels, storefront glazing and interior glass partitions share the same problem: the load is large, fragile and usually must travel through a finished building before it reaches its position. A spider crane addresses all three constraints at once — its crawler chassis folds through standard doorways, outriggers spread load without fixing to the structure, and the telescopic boom places panels at radii that telehandlers cannot reach indoors.

Calculate the real lifted load

Start from the panel, not the crane. Glass weight in kilograms is roughly: panel area (m²) × thickness (mm) × 2.5. A 3.0 × 1.8 m panel at 12 mm weighs about 162 kg; add the suction lifter frame, spreader beam and hook block, and the rigged load approaches 190 kg. That rigged load — not the bare panel — is the number you carry to the load chart, at the working radius you will actually lift at, not the maximum radius in the brochure.

Choose the suction lifter before the crane

The vacuum lifter determines panel orientation and safety margin. A two-pad lifter suits narrow panels lifted vertically; a four-pad frame with a spreader beam keeps tall panels flat during rotation and reduces edge stress. Confirm the lifter's rated capacity at the panel's actual surface area and weight — suction ratings fall quickly on textured or coated glass — and check the crane's auxiliary hydraulics or electric power supply matches the lifter's requirement.

Plan the route and outriggers together

Measure the route from unloading point to final position: doorway widths, corridor turns, floor transitions and elevator dimensions. Then plan the crane's standing position so the boom reaches the panel's final position without swinging through occupied areas. Outrigger spread must fit inside the working zone — on suspended slabs, confirm the structure can carry the distributed outrigger reaction loads before the crane enters the building.

Sequence the lift

A controlled glass lift runs in a fixed order: stage the panel on trestles within boom reach; verify vacuum on all pads before lifting off the trestles; lift 100–200 mm and hold to confirm load stability; rotate at low speed with the tag line controlling panel swing; place against setting blocks, not directly onto hard edges; release vacuum only after the panel is mechanically supported.

Frequently asked questions

What radius should I use when checking glass panel capacity?

The horizontal distance from the boom's slew centre to the panel's final hanging position. Capacity falls as radius increases, so check the chart value at the position where the panel will actually be placed, plus a margin for the suction frame and hook hardware.

Can a spider crane lift glass vertically and rotate it?

Yes. With a four-pad suction lifter and a rotating hook or manipulator, panels can be lifted vertical, rotated to horizontal for placement and flipped during installation — the sequence must be planned before the crane enters the building.

What power does the suction lifter need?

Most vacuum lifters draw from the crane's auxiliary hydraulic circuit or a separate electric vacuum unit with a reserve tank that holds vacuum during a power interruption. Confirm the power interface and reserve capacity at quotation.
