3-Ton vs 5-Ton Spider Crane: XZ03B, XZ03C and XZ05A Compared

Compare XZ03B, XZ03C and XZ05A rated load, boom length, access dimensions, weight and outrigger space before requesting a spider crane quotation.

Published October 4, 2026

Quick answer: A 3-ton or 5-ton label is a capacity class, not a guarantee at the required reach. Shortlist by the configuration-specific load chart, then check access width, outrigger space and transport mass. XZ03B and XZ03C have narrower listed product widths than XZ05A; XZ05A has a higher headline rating and longer listed boom.

When a 3-ton model belongs on the shortlist

Start with XZ03B or XZ03C when a 1,000 mm listed product width is relevant to the access route. XZ03B lists a 10.5 m fully extended boom and 11.3 m maximum lift height; XZ03C lists 11.7 m and 12.5 m respectively. The second model therefore deserves review for a different work position, but the longer boom does not establish greater capacity at a chosen radius. Check the actual chart for each candidate.

A narrow entrance is only one constraint. Measure turns, overhead obstructions and the final setup area. The listed maximum outrigger span for both models is 5,000 × 4,800 mm, substantially larger than their travel width. Record the available support area separately from the doorway measurement.

What changes when considering XZ05A

XZ05A lists 5,000 kg rated load, a 15.8 m fully extended boom and 17 m maximum lift height. Its listed product dimensions are 5,000 × 1,500 × 2,100 mm, with 7,200 kg machine weight. Compared with the 3-ton candidates, those figures change the route survey and delivery package as well as the capacity shortlist.

Do not choose it solely because its headline rating is larger. If the route cannot accept its configured width or the setup area cannot support the planned deployment, a larger machine does not resolve the project. Request the chart for the pickup and placement positions and check it with the proposed lifting gear.

Why maximum load and maximum reach cannot be combined

Use the load chart for the actual configuration and position, rather than combining headline maxima from different table rows. Our working-radius guide explains the information to collect. Manufacturer instructions and rated capacities govern the operation; the supplier’s product summary is a purchasing aid, not a lift plan.

For a US construction project, consult OSHA 1926.1417 on manufacturer procedures, accessible load charts and rated capacity. Project requirements elsewhere must be checked locally.

Build a quotation that can be compared

Send load weight and dimensions; lifting-gear weight; pickup and placement radii; required hook height; entrance width and height; available outrigger space; and site photos. State whether a jib, glass suction attachment or other option is required. Wireless remote control is listed as standard on these pages; other equipment must be identified separately in the quotation.

Ask for the exact configuration, load chart, drawings, shipping dimensions, packed mass, spare-parts scope and delivery term. Keep the same task assumptions when comparing suppliers. If the main task is personnel access, read spider crane versus aerial work platform before selecting equipment.
