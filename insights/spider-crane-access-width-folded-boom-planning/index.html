Spider Crane Access Width, Folded Boom and Indoor Lift Planning

Compare folded size, turning space, route limits and deployed working envelope before selecting a crawler spider crane.

Published September 24, 2026

Compare folded size, turning space, route limits and deployed working envelope before selecting a crawler spider crane. Use the checklist below to compare a quoted configuration with the demands of your site.

Map the complete route

Start at the delivery point and trace each doorway, ramp, lift, corridor and turn before reaching the lift position. Measure clear width and height after door frames, pipes and handrails, not only the nominal opening. A machine that fits straight through a gate may still be unable to turn or deploy at the destination.

Check the machine in transport position

The XZ02A, XZ03A and XZ05A product pages give a starting point for model comparison. Request the actual folded boom height, transport width, machine length and travel weight for the quoted configuration. Accessories, hook stowage and protective covers can change the effective envelope; confirm the transport drawing rather than relying on a staged image.

Plan setup as a second footprint

At the lifting position, include stabilizer spread, clearance for people to set pads, the proposed slew path and a way to move the load safely. The crane’s small crawler chassis does not represent its working footprint. Use the load chart that corresponds to the allowed outrigger positions and verify the support surface separately.

Indoor power and ventilation questions

Indoor work can constrain emissions, noise, power supply and cable routing. Ask which exact power configuration is available for the proposed machine and whether it suits the building. Do not assume every spider crane model has an electric option. Include floor loading, ventilation and fire procedures in the site review.

Send a dimensioned route drawing

Show the narrowest clear opening, turning corners, ramp gradient, ceiling obstructions, unloading point, lift position and pick radius. This lets the supplier respond with an appropriate model and a list of information still needed for a full lift plan.

Further technical reading

For general operating principles, see Maeda: compact spider crane dimensions and outriggers. Confirm all operating limits against the instructions for the actual equipment supplied.
