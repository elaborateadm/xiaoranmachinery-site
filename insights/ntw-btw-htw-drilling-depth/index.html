NTW, BTW and HTW Drilling Depth: How to Compare Core Rigs

Understand why NTW, BTW and HTW depth ratings differ, and compare exploration rigs by core diameter, formation and complete drilling system.

Updated September 24, 2026

Start with the required core size

Wireline core rigs often list separate depth ratings for BTW, NTW and HTW. These are different rod and core barrel systems. A smaller diameter string commonly reaches deeper under comparable conditions, so a single maximum depth number does not describe every core size.

Read each depth figure in its own column

The published FDX400 table lists 480 m BTW, 400 m NTW and 200 m HTW. The FDX600 lists 650 m BTW, 600 m NTW and 300 m HTW. These are model specifications rather than a promise of achieved depth on every site. Check your required core sample size before comparing the two rigs.

Consider formation and drilling method

Rock hardness, fracturing, borehole stability, water availability and drilling angle influence progress and tool wear. Ask the supplier to match bit, rods, casing, mud pump and winch capacity to the project. A rig depth rating alone cannot validate the full drilling plan.

Specify the complete package

For a useful quotation, send target depth by core size, geology, hole angle, site access, power requirements, climate and destination port. Request a written equipment list that separates standard items, optional tooling, consumables and spare parts.
