Wheeled vs Tracked Mini Skid Steer Loaders: Compare S and T Models

Compare paired S360/T360 through S490/T490 mini skid steer models by operating weight, rated load and published overall dimensions.

Published September 29, 2026

A wheel-versus-track decision is easier when the two machines are compared at the same rated load. The supplied brochure lists paired S-series wheeled and T-series tracked mini loaders. The figures below describe the brochure configurations, not every engine or attachment option.

Compare like-sized model pairs

The S and T versions often have similar rated loads but different operating weights and overall dimensions. Check the exact width with the proposed bucket: the overall dimensions below include the brochure's stated configuration.

Dimensions are length × width × height in millimetres. A larger model number does not always mean a larger rated load: compare the stated figure for the actual machine.

When wheels may suit the route

On a firm, prepared surface, the wheeled S-series may be a practical starting point for repetitive travel. Record pavement type, turning space, slopes and whether the work crosses finished surfaces. Tyre selection and surface protection should be confirmed for the exact configuration. Compare S480 with S460 if the job requires more rated load within a similar published length.

When tracks merit a closer look

Tracked machines can be considered for loose or uneven terrain, but a track does not make unstable ground safe. Inspect ground bearing conditions, edges and the route from delivery point to work area. The T480 and T460 are both 1120 mm wide in the brochure's overall dimensions; the smaller T360 lists 1050 mm. See our soft-ground planning guide for the site questions to ask.

Confirm the working configuration

Ask for the selected powertrain, bucket or attachment width, machine transport mass, hydraulic requirements, service parts and the applicable operating manual. Rated load and maximum load are separate brochure fields: do not treat the maximum figure as a routine working rating. Our rated-load and attachment guide explains this distinction.
